Abstract

Geographic atrophy (GA) in age-related macular degeneration expands irreversibly over months to years, and its future extent is what clinicians need to plan monitoring and treatment. We introduce GAP-INR, a conditional implicit neural representation that treats each patient-eye's longitudinal fundus autofluorescence (FAF) imaging and GA segmentation as samples of one continuous field.

A single SIREN decoder is shared across the entire cohort and modulated per eye by a learned latent code, and per visit by a temporal condition, applied through FiLM. From a query coordinate, the decoder jointly predicts FAF intensity and GA class probability. At test time the decoder is frozen and only a new eye's latent is fit to its observed visits (up to four); advancing the temporal condition beyond the last visit then forecasts a scan that was never acquired.

Against ImageFlowNet and its reported baselines (I2SB-UNet, T-UNet), and against a naive copy-forward control, GAP-INR reconstructs held-out visits more accurately and produces lesion-area trajectories that track each eye's individual growth curve — including under genuine extrapolation — more faithfully than population-level curve fits such as linear or cubic B-spline interpolation.

Overview of the GAP-INR architecture

A single SIREN decoder is shared across every patient-eye in the dataset, modulated by two signals:
(a) Training: subject i's visits (t1…t4) supervise the shared decoder, FiLM-modulated by the eye's latent code zi, the temporal condition t, and age at visit. Reconstruction (fθfaf) and segmentation (fθseg) heads read out from the shared trunk.
(b) Test-time optimisation: for a new subject k, the decoder is frozen and only the latent zk is fit on the available visits (t1…t3) by backpropagating the reconstruction and segmentation loss into the latent alone. Querying t4 then predicts a visit that was never acquired.

Per-Eye Lesion Area Trajectories

One scatter panel per test eye: ground-truth lesion area at each real visit against GAP-INR's predicted trajectory, alongside copy-forward, linear interpolation/extrapolation, and cubic B-spline fits.

Why monotonicity matters. GA is anatomically irreversible — once tissue atrophies, the lesion does not shrink. Curve fits like linear or cubic B-spline have no mechanism to enforce this and can predict area decreasing between visits; a faithful model's predicted trajectory should stay non-decreasing along each eye's own timeline, not just accurate at the sampled points.

Latent Capacity Ablation

Channel count and spatial grid size of the per-eye latent, swept jointly.

The per-eye latent is a grid of shape (channels, H, W). Too coarse and the shared decoder cannot carry enough per-eye detail; too large and the latent risks memorising rather than generalising across visits. The heatmap reports held-out performance across the sweep of channel count (rows) and spatial grid size (columns); see the paper for exact values and the metric definition used.
